Research

Coral reefs are natural breakwaters: they build islands, feed shorelines with sediment, and shelter hundreds of millions of people from waves. My work asks how these landforms respond when the pressures of development, sediment, and rising seas meet.

Reef island shorelines

Why do reef island coastlines grow, shrink, and shift? I track shoreline change across Southeast Asia with field surveys and satellite remote sensing to untangle the biophysical drivers behind it.

Reefs under pressure

Using high-resolution hydrodynamic-sediment modelling, I quantify how coastal reclamation redirects currents and fine sediment over urban reefs, starting with the reefs of southern Singapore.

Vulnerable coasts & sea level

From region-wide coastal vulnerability mapping of Southeast Asia to sea-level change from tide gauges and altimetry, I put reef science in the service of coastal resilience.

PhD thesis

Transforming Coasts: Natural and Anthropogenic Drivers of Change in Southeast Asia

Doctor of Philosophy, Asian School of the Environment and Earth Observatory of Singapore, Nanyang Technological University. Defended 28 August 2026. Supervised by Asst Prof Kyle Morgan (NTU), Prof Paul S. Kench (NUS), and Assoc Prof Adam D. Switzer (NTU).

Coral reef islands are built from the sediment their own reefs produce. That makes their shorelines unusually sensitive to anything that changes how water moves and where sediment settles. The thesis asks what actually drives that change across Southeast Asia, working at three scales.

  1. Local process

    A high-resolution hydrodynamic and sediment transport model of Singapore's Southern Islands quantifies how decades of land reclamation have redirected currents and fine sediment across urban reefs.

  2. Regional pattern

    Shoreline change on 110 reef islands across ten Southeast Asian marine ecoregions, mapped from satellite imagery between 2004 and 2024 and tested against island morphometry, asking whether relationships established on remote atolls hold in a more heterogeneous and more pressured setting.

  3. Regional vulnerability

    An ecosystem-based coastal vulnerability assessment covering Southeast Asia, built with the InVEST coastal protection model, which treats reefs, mangroves and seagrass as protective assets rather than background.

The thesis will be linked here once it is deposited in NTU's DR-NTU repository.

About

I grew up in Yogyakarta, Indonesia, and found my way to the ocean through oceanography: first riding waves of data on renewable energy and sea surface temperature, then following sea level across three continents, and finally arriving at the reefs I now study. Along the way I served as Maritime Ambassador (Duta Maritim) for Yogyakarta in 2017 and worked in disaster management, experiences that keep my science anchored to the coastlines people live on.
